WEEK ONE HOW GOD SPEAKS Understanding the Biblical Ways God Communicates With His People Key Scripture: Hebrews 1:1–2 Supporting Scripture: John 10:27 The Foundation of Hearing God The desire to hear God begins with understanding that God has always been a communicating God. Throughout the Bible, the Lord reveals His character, His will, His instructions, His promises, and His purposes through communication with humanity. God has never been distant from His creation or indifferent toward the lives of those who seek Him. Hebrews 1:1–2 explains that God spoke to the fathers through the prophets in many portions and many ways throughout previous generations. In these last days, God has spoken through His Son. This passage establishes an important foundation for our study: God has communicated in different ways throughout Scripture, yet every revelation of God ultimately points toward His character, His truth, and His redemptive purpose. The Bible records God speaking audibly, communicating through dreams and visions, giving instruction through prophets, revealing truth through Scripture, guiding individuals through circumstances, and leading His people through the work of His Spirit. These accounts demonstrate the variety of ways God communicated while reminding us that discernment requires a biblical understanding of His character and purposes. Our goal is not to become fascinated with methods of communication. Our goal is to know the God who communicates. 1. God Speaks Through His Word The written Word of God provides the foundation for understanding His voice. Scripture reveals God’s character, His holiness, His wisdom, His expectations, His promises, and His will concerning the lives of His people. Second Timothy 3:16–17 teaches that all Scripture is inspired by God and profitable for teaching, reproof, correction, and instruction in righteousness. The Word equips believers for faithful living and establishes a foundation for spiritual understanding. The importance of Scripture cannot be overstated. Personal impressions, emotions, dreams, circumstances, and the words of others must never become greater authorities than the written Word of God. When someone believes God has spoken, Scripture provides a foundation for examining that belief. God’s character does not change, and His truth does not contradict His own revelation. Psalm 119:105 describes the Word as a lamp unto our feet and a light unto our path. The image communicates guidance for the journey ahead. God does not merely provide information. His Word illuminates the path of faithful living and helps us understand how to walk according to His truth. The more familiar we become with Scripture, the more deeply we understand the character of the God who speaks. Key Scriptures: • 2 Timothy 3:16–17 • Psalm 119:105 • Psalm 119:130 • John 17:17 • Hebrews 4:12 Reflection A person who desires to recognize God’s voice must become familiar with God’s Word. Scripture establishes the boundaries of discernment and provides the truth against which every impression must be examined. ⸻ 2. God Speaks Through the Holy Ghost The Holy Ghost plays a vital role in the spiritual life of believers. Jesus promised that the Spirit would guide His followers into truth, remind them of His teachings, and help them understand what He had spoken. John 14:26 describes the Comforter, the Holy Ghost, teaching and bringing the words of Jesus to remembrance. John 16:13 explains that the Spirit of truth guides into truth. The leading of the Holy Ghost is not separated from the Word of God. The Spirit who inspired Scripture will never lead a person contrary to the truth revealed in Scripture. The Holy Ghost may bring conviction concerning sin, prompt a believer toward prayer, provide wisdom during a decision, strengthen someone facing difficulty, or direct a person toward a responsibility or opportunity. Spiritual sensitivity requires a heart willing to respond to the Spirit’s work. Conviction and condemnation must also be understood carefully. Conviction draws us toward repentance, truth, and restoration. God’s correction may be uncomfortable, yet His purpose is to bring us into alignment with His will. Shame that produces hopelessness and spiritual paralysis does not reflect the redemptive character of God. Romans 8:14 teaches that those who are led by the Spirit of God are sons of God. Spiritual leading is connected to relationship, identity, and a life surrendered to Him. Learning to recognize the Holy Ghost’s leading requires prayer, obedience, biblical knowledge, humility, and spiritual maturity. Key Scriptures: • John 14:26 • John 16:13 • Romans 8:14 • Galatians 5:16–25 • 1 Corinthians 2:10–12 Reflection The leading of the Holy Ghost is not merely an extraordinary experience. Spiritual guidance may occur through conviction, wisdom, understanding, a reminder of Scripture, or a growing clarity concerning the direction God desires us to take. ⸻ 3. God Speaks Through Prayer and Communion Prayer is more than presenting requests before God. Prayer is communion with Him. Through prayer, believers express worship, surrender, gratitude, confession, intercession, and dependence upon the Lord. A healthy prayer life involves speaking to God while also allowing our hearts to become quiet, attentive, and receptive to His truth. Samuel’s experience in 1 Samuel 3 demonstrates the importance of spiritual attentiveness. As a young boy serving in the house of the Lord, Samuel heard his name called and initially did not recognize the source. Eli eventually instructed him to respond, “Speak, Lord; for thy servant heareth.” Samuel’s response reveals a posture that remains essential to spiritual discernment: willingness to listen and willingness to obey. Prayer also helps us recognize the difference between seeking God’s direction and attempting to persuade God to approve our own direction. Sometimes we enter prayer with a decision already made, hoping for confirmation. Genuine surrender requires the willingness to receive God’s wisdom even when His direction differs from our expectations. Philippians 4:6–7 encourages believers to bring their requests before God through prayer and supplication with thanksgiving. Prayer brings our concerns before the Lord while allowing His peace to guard our hearts and minds. Prayer creates space for our desires, fears, and expectations to be brought into the presence of God. Through consistent communion with Him, our hearts become increasingly shaped by His truth. Key Scriptures: • 1 Samuel 3:1–10 • Philippians 4:6–7 • Jeremiah 33:3 • Psalm 25:4–5 • James 1:5 Reflection A listening heart is a surrendered heart. Prayer helps us become attentive to God while releasing the need to control the outcome of our decisions. ⸻ 4. God Speaks Through Godly Wisdom and Counsel Throughout Scripture, God demonstrates the value of wisdom, instruction, and counsel. Spiritual discernment does not require believers to isolate themselves from others or assume that every decision must be made independently. Proverbs 11:14 teaches that safety is found through an abundance of counselors. Proverbs 15:22 explains that plans are established through counsel. God may use spiritually mature individuals to provide wisdom, encouragement, correction, perspective, and confirmation. Pastors, teachers, mentors, and faithful believers may help us examine a situation through the lens of Scripture. Seeking counsel does not mean surrendering personal responsibility for spiritual discernment. A person’s position, influence, confidence, or spiritual reputation does not automatically make every statement accurate. Counsel must also be evaluated through Scripture, wisdom, character, and prayer. Healthy spiritual counsel encourages a person toward truth, godliness, humility, responsibility, and a closer relationship with Jesus Christ. Godly counsel does not manipulate, control, intimidate, or demand unquestioning allegiance to a human voice. The purpose of counsel is not to replace our relationship with God. The purpose is to help us grow in wisdom and faithfully understand how biblical truth applies to our lives. Key Scriptures: • Proverbs 11:14 • Proverbs 15:22 • Proverbs 19:20 • Acts 15:1–29 • Colossians 3:16 Reflection Godly counsel can help us recognize blind spots, evaluate our decisions, and receive wisdom we may not have considered. Spiritual maturity includes the humility to listen while retaining responsibility to examine everything through God’s Word. ⸻ 5. God May Speak Through Circumstances Throughout the Bible, circumstances sometimes played a role in revealing God’s direction. Opportunities, unexpected encounters, timing, deliverance, provision, and obstacles appear throughout the accounts of God’s dealings with His people. The life of Joseph provides a powerful example. Joseph experienced betrayal, slavery, imprisonment, and eventually elevation into a position of leadership in Egypt. Looking back upon the events of his life, Joseph recognized God’s providence and purpose. Genesis 50:20 reveals Joseph’s understanding that God had worked through circumstances that others intended for harm, bringing about preservation and provision. Circumstances can provide meaningful insight, yet circumstances alone do not establish God’s direction. An open door does not automatically mean God has approved a particular decision. A closed door does not always mean a person has missed God’s will. Difficulties may occur during obedience, while opportunities may arise that require careful evaluation. Discernment requires us to consider circumstances alongside Scripture, prayer, wisdom, timing, character, and the fruit produced by our decisions. Rather than treating every event as a sign requiring immediate interpretation, believers can trust God to provide wisdom as they faithfully walk through the circumstances of life. Key Scriptures: • Genesis 50:20 • Acts 16:6–10 • Romans 8:28 • Proverbs 3:5–6 • James 4:13–15 Reflection Circumstances may contribute to our understanding of God’s direction, yet our confidence must remain in God rather than in our interpretation of events. ⸻ 6. God Spoke Through Dreams, Visions, and Audible Communication The Bible records occasions when God communicated through dreams, visions, and audible speech. These accounts demonstrate that God is sovereign and capable of communicating in ways beyond ordinary human understanding. God spoke to Abraham, Moses, Samuel, the prophets, and others throughout Scripture. He communicated through dreams to Joseph, Daniel, and individuals connected with the unfolding biblical narrative. Acts 2:17 describes the prophetic promise concerning dreams and visions. These biblical accounts should be approached with reverence and discernment. The existence of dreams and visions in Scripture does not mean every dream carries spiritual significance or every unusual experience represents communication from God. Dreams may reflect ordinary experiences, thoughts, emotions, concerns, or memories. A spiritual impression or unusual experience should never be accepted unquestioningly or elevated above Scripture. When someone believes a dream, vision, or audible experience carries spiritual significance, the message must be examined carefully. Does the message agree with Scripture? Does the message reflect the character of Christ? Does the message encourage godliness and truth? Is there wisdom in seeking mature counsel? God’s ability to communicate through extraordinary means does not eliminate our responsibility to exercise discernment. Key Scriptures: • Genesis 15:1 • Numbers 12:6–8 • 1 Samuel 3:1–10 • Daniel 2:19 • Acts 2:17 • Hebrews 1:1–2 Reflection Biblical accounts of extraordinary communication remind us of God’s sovereignty. Discernment keeps our focus upon truth, humility, and the character of the Lord rather than upon extraordinary experiences themselves. ⸻ 7. God Speaks With Purpose Throughout Scripture, God’s communication carries purpose. He speaks to reveal truth, establish covenant, provide instruction, correct His people, call individuals into service, offer promises, warn against danger, and reveal His redemptive plan. God’s voice is never intended to lead us away from His character. His communication calls people toward truth, holiness, faithfulness, obedience, love, and relationship. When we desire to hear God, we must also examine our willingness to respond. A person may desire guidance while remaining unwilling to surrender personal preferences. Genuine discernment requires a willingness to obey God’s truth even when obedience requires patience, sacrifice, humility, or a change of direction. Jesus demonstrated complete submission to the Father’s will. In the Garden of Gethsemane, He expressed the weight of what lay before Him while surrendering to the Father’s purpose. His example reminds us that surrender is central to our relationship with God. The question is not simply, “Lord, what are You saying?” The deeper question is, “Lord, am I willing to follow You?” God’s guidance is a gift, yet our response remains a responsibility. Hearing must lead toward faithfulness. Key Scriptures: • John 10:27 • Luke 22:42 • James 1:22 • Deuteronomy 30:19–20 • Matthew 7:24 ⸻ Bringing the Lesson Together God communicates through His Word, the work of the Holy Ghost, prayer, godly counsel, circumstances, and the extraordinary means recorded throughout Scripture. These forms of communication must never be separated from the biblical foundation of truth. The most important preparation for discerning God’s voice is not searching for unusual experiences. The most important preparation is cultivating a relationship with Him. We become familiar with His character through Scripture. We develop spiritual attentiveness through prayer. We grow in wisdom through obedience and godly counsel. We learn patience by trusting His timing. We develop discernment through spiritual maturity. Hearing God is not about achieving spiritual superiority or proving that we possess special insight. Hearing God is about faithfully following Jesus Christ. As we begin this seven-week journey, may our hearts be prepared to listen, our minds be grounded in Scripture, and our lives be surrendered to the Lord.
