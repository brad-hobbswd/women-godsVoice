A Seven-Week Bible Study for Women · Week 2

Recognizing His Voice

Learning the Character of the Shepherd and Developing Spiritual Familiarity

✦ ❧ ✦

Hear · Discern · Respond

Key Scripture: John 10:1–5, 14, 27

Supporting Scripture: John 20:11–18

Introduction: Familiarity Comes Through Relationship

Understanding how God speaks is foundational. Recognizing His voice requires something deeper: relationship.

Jesus described His followers as sheep who recognize the voice of their shepherd. His illustration reveals an important truth about spiritual discernment. Recognition develops through familiarity. Sheep do not follow every voice calling from the field. They respond to the voice they know.

John 10:27 declares:

“My sheep hear My voice, and I know them, and they follow Me.”

The relationship between Jesus and His followers is personal. He knows His sheep, and His sheep learn to recognize Him.

As women seeking spiritual growth, we may desire clear direction concerning decisions, relationships, responsibilities, ministry opportunities, or seasons of life. Questions may arise concerning whether a thought represents God’s leading, personal desires, emotional reactions, or the influence of someone else.

Recognizing God’s voice does not come through a formula. Spiritual familiarity develops as we consistently spend time with Him, study His Word, pray, worship, obey, and grow in understanding His character.

The more deeply we know the Shepherd, the more our discernment becomes shaped by who He is.

The Voice We Recognize Belongs to the Shepherd

In John 10, Jesus uses the illustration of a shepherd and his sheep to describe His relationship with His followers.

John 10:3–5 explains that the sheep hear the shepherd’s voice, recognize him, and follow him. They do not follow the voice of a stranger because they are unfamiliar with that voice.

The emphasis is not on the sheep possessing extraordinary abilities. Their recognition develops through relationship and familiarity.

Jesus is not simply someone we turn to when we need an answer. He is our Shepherd. He leads, protects, teaches, corrects, and cares for His people.

Recognition develops through walking with Him.

A woman who desires to discern God’s voice must cultivate a consistent relationship with the Lord rather than relying exclusively upon moments of crisis. Prayer cannot become something reserved for emergencies. Scripture cannot become something opened only when a decision must be made.

Our relationship with Jesus must become part of the rhythm of our lives.

As we walk with the Shepherd, we become increasingly familiar with His character, His truth, His ways, and the direction He provides through His Word.

Biblical Example

Mary Magdalene

In John 20:11–18, Mary Magdalene stood outside the tomb of Jesus, weeping. During her grief, she encountered the risen Lord but did not initially recognize Him.

Jesus called her by name.

When He spoke, Mary recognized Him and responded, “Rabboni!”

The encounter demonstrates the personal nature of the relationship between Jesus and His followers. The Shepherd knows His sheep. He is not distant from their grief, confusion, or sorrow.

Mary’s recognition came through the personal encounter of the risen Christ calling her by name.

There are seasons when emotions may become overwhelming, circumstances may be difficult to understand, or our hearts may carry heavy burdens. Such seasons do not mean God has abandoned us. We remain known by Him, loved by Him, and invited into relationship with Him.

Key Principle

Recognizing the voice of God begins with knowing the Shepherd, not merely seeking an answer.

Recognizing His Voice Requires Knowing His Character

We cannot separate the voice of God from the character of God.

God is holy, righteous, truthful, merciful, faithful, wise, loving, and just. His leading reflects His nature. His voice will never contradict His Word or lead us into sin.

The more we study Scripture, the more clearly we understand the character of the Lord. Scripture reveals how He responds to sin, extends mercy, corrects, leads, and calls people toward obedience.

Discernment becomes difficult when our understanding of God’s character remains incomplete.

Sometimes a woman may mistake condemnation for conviction. She may believe God is rejecting her when He is actually correcting her. She may interpret correction as punishment or assume a difficult season means she has lost God’s favor.

Understanding God’s character helps us discern His dealings with us.

Conviction and Condemnation

The Holy Ghost brings conviction that leads toward repentance, truth, correction, and restoration.

Conviction identifies something requiring attention and draws us toward God.

Condemnation produces hopelessness, shame, and the belief that there is no way forward.

Romans 8:1 declares that there is no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.

God’s correction is purposeful. He desires our spiritual growth and restoration. Conviction may challenge us deeply, yet His purpose is to bring us into alignment with truth.

His correction does not excuse sin, nor does His grace encourage compromise. His grace empowers us to grow, repent, and walk faithfully.

Biblical Example

The Woman at the Well

In John 4:1–30, Jesus encountered a Samaritan woman whose life carried personal history, relational experiences, and spiritual questions.

Jesus spoke truth concerning her life while also revealing Himself as the Messiah.

He did not avoid the truth. He addressed her circumstances while offering the living water that only He could provide.

The encounter demonstrates that Jesus knows us completely. Our past, questions, circumstances, and personal struggles are not hidden from Him.

Recognizing His voice requires understanding that His truth and His mercy work together. He brings truth into our lives while inviting us toward transformation.

Key Principle

God’s voice reflects His character. His truth corrects us, His wisdom guides us, and His grace draws us toward transformation.

His Voice Will Agree With His Word

Spiritual familiarity cannot be separated from Scripture.

A person may sincerely believe God is speaking, yet sincerity alone does not establish the accuracy of an impression. Our thoughts, feelings, expectations, and desires can influence our interpretation of spiritual experiences.

God’s written Word provides the foundation for recognizing His voice.

Psalm 119:11 teaches the importance of hiding God’s Word in our hearts. When Scripture becomes deeply rooted within us, truth shapes our thinking and helps us evaluate what we hear.

The Word of God helps us recognize direction that reflects His truth and identify thoughts that contradict His commandments, principles, or character.

A woman seeking God’s direction concerning a relationship, ministry opportunity, personal decision, or future season must remain grounded in Scripture.

We should never use a supposed word from God to justify dishonesty, disobedience, manipulation, bitterness, compromise, or actions that contradict biblical truth.

God’s leading will never require us to disobey His Word.

Biblical Example

Jesus and the Word

In Matthew 4:1–11, Jesus faced temptation in the wilderness. Each time Satan presented temptation, Jesus responded with the Word of God.

Jesus demonstrated the importance of knowing Scripture and remaining grounded in truth.

The Word was not merely something Jesus quoted. His response demonstrated a life aligned with the truth of the Father.

Our familiarity with Scripture equips us to recognize truth when competing voices attempt to influence our decisions.

Key Principle

The more deeply God’s Word is established in our hearts, the better equipped we become to recognize what aligns with His truth.

Competing Voices Can Affect Our Discernment

Recognizing God’s voice requires understanding that not every voice influencing our thoughts comes from God.

We may experience the influence of:

  • Fear
  • Anxiety
  • Personal desires
  • Emotional reactions
  • Past experiences
  • The opinions of others
  • Pressure to make a decision
  • Impatience
  • Expectations concerning how God should respond

These influences can become especially strong during difficult seasons.

A woman may desire a relationship so deeply that she begins interpreting every interaction as confirmation. Another may fear disappointment so strongly that she struggles to trust any direction. Someone may receive advice from several people and become confused by conflicting opinions.

Discernment requires us to slow down and examine what is influencing our hearts.

We must learn to ask whether we are responding to God’s direction or reacting to fear, pressure, desire, disappointment, or personal expectations.

God does not require us to make every decision during a moment of emotional pressure. We can pray, seek Scripture, receive wise counsel, and allow time for clarity.

Biblical Example

Elijah

In 1 Kings 19:9–13, Elijah experienced a difficult season marked by exhaustion, fear, and discouragement.

God met Elijah during this season. The passage describes a powerful wind, an earthquake, and fire. Following these events, Elijah encountered a still, small voice.

The passage reminds us that God is not limited to dramatic manifestations. His communication may come in ways requiring attentiveness rather than spectacle.

Elijah’s experience also reminds us that emotional and physical exhaustion can affect our perspective. God ministered to Elijah’s needs and continued guiding him.

Sometimes spiritual discernment requires recognizing that we need rest, prayer, time in Scripture, and renewed strength before making important decisions.

Key Principle

Not every powerful emotion or dramatic experience represents God’s direction. Discernment requires attentiveness, patience, and a heart grounded in truth.

Familiarity With His Voice Develops Through Spiritual Maturity

Recognizing God’s voice is not a competition. Spiritual maturity develops over time through relationship, learning, prayer, Scripture, obedience, and experience.

Some women may have years of experience in prayer and Scripture. Others may be developing their spiritual understanding for the first time. Every woman has an opportunity to grow.

We should never measure our spiritual maturity by comparing our experiences with another person’s testimony.

Someone else’s dream, vision, prophetic experience, or testimony does not establish the standard by which God must communicate with us.

God knows each of His children personally. Our responsibility is to remain faithful in the relationship He has given us.

Spiritual maturity involves learning to recognize truth, respond to correction, exercise wisdom, remain humble, and follow the Lord faithfully.

The more consistently we walk with God, the more our thinking becomes shaped by His Word and our responses reflect His character.

Recognition grows through faithful relationship.

Biblical Example

The Disciples

Throughout the Gospels, the disciples spent time with Jesus. They listened to His teaching, witnessed His works, asked questions, received correction, and learned through experience.

Their understanding did not develop instantly. They sometimes misunderstood His teachings, responded with fear, and struggled to understand His purpose.

Jesus continued teaching them.

Their relationship demonstrates that spiritual growth involves learning, correction, and continued discipleship.

We should allow ourselves to grow without becoming discouraged by what we do not yet understand.

Key Principle

Spiritual familiarity develops through a lifelong relationship with Jesus, not through one extraordinary experience.

Bringing the Lesson Together

Recognizing God’s voice begins with relationship.

We learn the voice of the Shepherd by becoming familiar with His character, studying His Word, developing a consistent prayer life, responding to the leading of the Holy Ghost, and growing in spiritual maturity.

We must remain aware of competing influences that may affect our understanding. Fear, desire, emotional pressure, personal expectations, and the opinions of others can cloud discernment.

God’s voice remains consistent with His Word and His character.

We do not need to force an answer or manufacture an experience. We can trust the Lord to lead us as we faithfully seek Him.

The goal is not simply to become women who believe they can hear God. The goal is to become women who know Him deeply, recognize truth, respond faithfully, and follow the Shepherd with confidence.

Hear His voice. Discern His leading. Respond with obedience.

Personal Reflection and Preparation for Class

Prayerfully consider the following questions before our gathering:

  • What does the relationship between the Shepherd and His sheep teach us about recognizing God’s voice?
  • How does knowing the character of God help us discern His direction?
  • What role does Scripture play in recognizing and testing what we believe we hear?
  • Which competing voices most often influence your thoughts or decisions: fear, anxiety, personal desires, other people’s opinions, impatience, or past experiences?
  • How can consistent prayer, Scripture, worship, and obedience help develop spiritual familiarity?
  • What does spiritual maturity look like when we are uncertain about God’s direction?
  • How can we remain confident in our relationship with God while continuing to grow in discernment?

Closing Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You for being our Shepherd and for knowing each of us personally. Thank You for loving us, guiding us, correcting us, and calling us into a deeper relationship with You.

Teach us to become more familiar with Your character and Your Word. Help us recognize Your truth when competing voices attempt to influence our hearts and decisions.

Give us wisdom when emotions become overwhelming, patience when answers are delayed, and discernment when direction is uncertain.

Help us become women who seek You faithfully, listen with humility, trust Your Word, and follow Your leading with confidence.

Continue developing spiritual maturity within us. May our relationship with You become deeper, our faith become stronger, and our lives reflect Your character.

We desire to know You more deeply and follow You more faithfully.

In Jesus’ name, Amen.
