DISCERNING THE VOICE OF GOD WEEK THREE: THE HOLY GHOST AND DISCERNMENT Understanding the Spirit’s Work in Guidance, Conviction, Wisdom, and Direction Key Scripture: John 16:13 Supporting Scripture: Romans 8:14, Galatians 5:16–25 ⸻ Introduction: The Spirit Who Leads Us Into Truth Recognizing the voice of God involves more than becoming familiar with the character of the Shepherd. God has given His Spirit to lead, teach, strengthen, convict, and guide His people. The Holy Ghost is not simply an emotional experience or a spiritual feeling. He is the Spirit of God working within the believer, bringing understanding, revealing truth, strengthening faith, and helping us live according to the will of God. Jesus promised His disciples that the Spirit of Truth would guide them. John 16:13 reveals that the Holy Ghost would guide them into truth and communicate what He received. As women seeking to grow in spiritual discernment, we must understand the work of the Holy Ghost and learn to recognize the difference between spiritual leading, personal emotions, our own thoughts, and outside influences. The Holy Ghost does not lead us away from Scripture. He brings us into alignment with truth, reveals the importance of obedience, and develops the character of Christ within us. Spiritual discernment is not simply recognizing something supernatural. Discernment involves understanding what is true, recognizing what is spiritually beneficial, examining what influences us, and responding faithfully to the direction of God. Throughout this lesson, we will examine the work of the Holy Ghost and how His leading develops within a woman’s daily walk with God. ⸻ 1. The Holy Ghost Leads Us Into Truth Jesus described the Holy Ghost as the Spirit of Truth. John 16:13 teaches that the Spirit of Truth guides into truth. His work is not to create confusion or lead us into deception. He directs us toward what is consistent with the truth of God. The Holy Ghost helps us understand Scripture, recognize truth, and develop spiritual wisdom. He brings God’s Word to our remembrance and helps us apply biblical principles to our lives. There may be moments when a Scripture suddenly becomes clear during prayer. A passage we have read many times may speak directly to a situation we are facing. We may recognize that an attitude, decision, or behavior needs to change. The Holy Ghost works through the truth of God’s Word to bring understanding and transformation. We must remain careful not to confuse our own interpretations with the voice of God. A strong feeling, sudden thought, or personal impression does not automatically establish divine direction. Spiritual understanding must be examined through Scripture, prayer, wisdom, and the character of Christ. Biblical Example: The Disciples and the Promise of the Spirit In John 14:26, Jesus promised that the Holy Ghost would teach His disciples and bring His words to their remembrance. The disciples had spent time with Jesus, heard His teaching, and witnessed His ministry. Following His departure, the Holy Ghost would continue working within them, helping them understand and remember His teachings. The promise reveals the importance of the Holy Ghost in developing spiritual understanding. God does not leave His people without guidance. He has given His Word and His Spirit to help us grow in truth. Key Principle: The Holy Ghost leads us into truth and helps us understand and apply the Word of God. ⸻ 2. The Holy Ghost Brings Conviction and Correction One of the important works of the Holy Ghost is conviction. Conviction reveals areas of our lives requiring attention. The Spirit may bring awareness of sin, an attitude that needs correction, a relationship requiring wisdom, or a behavior that does not reflect the character of Christ. Conviction is an invitation to respond to God. When the Holy Ghost brings correction, we should not resist His work or attempt to justify what He is revealing. A teachable heart is willing to examine itself, repent, receive correction, and move forward in obedience. Conviction does not mean God has abandoned us. His correction demonstrates His desire for our spiritual growth. Hebrews 12:6 teaches that the Lord disciplines those He loves. His correction is purposeful and intended to produce growth, righteousness, and maturity. There is an important distinction between conviction and condemnation. Conviction identifies what needs to change and draws us toward repentance and restoration. Condemnation attempts to convince us that we are hopeless, rejected, or beyond the reach of God’s grace. Romans 8:1 reminds us that there is no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus. The Holy Ghost does not excuse sin, but He works to bring us out of sin and into a life that reflects God’s holiness. Biblical Example: Peter After His Denial In Luke 22:54–62, Peter denied knowing Jesus three times. When the rooster crowed, Peter remembered the words of Jesus and went out weeping bitterly. Peter experienced the weight of his failure. His response revealed sorrow over what he had done. Following the resurrection, Jesus restored Peter and gave him renewed responsibility in ministry. John 21:15–19 records this restoration. Peter’s life demonstrates that conviction, repentance, and restoration are part of God’s work in a believer’s life. The Holy Ghost brings us toward truth and repentance, not toward permanent despair. Key Principle: Conviction brings us into alignment with God and leads us toward repentance, restoration, and spiritual growth. ⸻ 3. The Holy Ghost Gives Wisdom and Direction The Holy Ghost helps believers navigate decisions, responsibilities, and circumstances with spiritual wisdom. Romans 8:14 teaches that those who are led by the Spirit of God are sons of God. Being led by the Spirit involves a life that remains responsive to God. The leading of the Holy Ghost is not limited to extraordinary experiences. God may guide us through Scripture, prayer, wisdom, conviction, circumstances, and understanding developed through spiritual maturity. A woman may be seeking direction concerning her family, employment, ministry, relationships, or personal responsibilities. During those seasons, the Holy Ghost can help her recognize biblical principles, exercise wisdom, and remain sensitive to God’s direction. Spiritual leading does not eliminate our responsibility to think carefully, seek wise counsel, evaluate circumstances, and make decisions prayerfully. God’s guidance does not require us to abandon wisdom. Sometimes the direction of God involves taking a step of faith. At other times, His leading may involve waiting, gathering information, seeking counsel, or recognizing that a decision requires greater understanding. We must resist the temptation to label every thought or opportunity as divine direction. The Holy Ghost leads us in ways consistent with Scripture and the character of Jesus Christ. Biblical Example: Philip and the Ethiopian Eunuch Acts 8:26–40 records how the Spirit directed Philip toward the Ethiopian eunuch. Philip received direction to approach the chariot. When he encountered the man reading Scripture, Philip asked whether he understood what he was reading. The conversation led to Philip explaining the gospel and the man receiving baptism. The example demonstrates the Holy Ghost’s ability to direct a believer toward an opportunity to minister. Philip responded to the direction he received, remained attentive to the situation, and used Scripture to explain the truth. The Spirit’s leading and the Word of God worked together. Key Principle: The Holy Ghost guides us toward God’s purposes while helping us exercise biblical wisdom and faithful obedience. ⸻ 4. The Holy Ghost Develops Spiritual Discernment Discernment involves recognizing the difference between truth and error, wisdom and foolishness, spiritual maturity and immaturity, and godly direction and personal influence. The Holy Ghost helps develop spiritual discernment within us. However, discernment requires growth, instruction, and a willingness to examine what we believe. First Corinthians 2:10–12 teaches that the Spirit searches and reveals the things of God. Spiritual understanding allows believers to recognize truths that cannot be understood through human reasoning alone. This does not mean spiritual discernment replaces common sense, education, wisdom, or careful thinking. Rather, the Holy Ghost helps us view life through the truth and wisdom of God. Discernment is especially important when we receive advice, hear a prophetic word, experience a strong impression, or encounter a situation that appears spiritually significant. We must examine what we hear. First John 4:1 instructs believers to test the spirits because not every spiritual claim originates from God. The Holy Ghost does not require us to become suspicious of everything. He develops within us a mature ability to recognize truth and respond wisely. Discernment also involves humility. We must remain willing to acknowledge when we misunderstood a situation, interpreted an impression incorrectly, or allowed our emotions to influence our judgment. Biblical Example: The Church at Antioch Acts 13:1–3 describes believers at Antioch who were ministering to the Lord and fasting when the Holy Ghost directed them to separate Barnabas and Saul for the work to which He had called them. The church responded through prayer, fasting, and obedience. The example demonstrates spiritual discernment within a community of believers. Direction was received, considered, and followed within a setting of worship, spiritual maturity, and ministry. God’s leading was not separated from the life of the church. Key Principle: Spiritual discernment grows through Scripture, prayer, spiritual maturity, humility, and responsiveness to the Holy Ghost. ⸻ 5. The Spirit’s Leading Produces Spiritual Fruit The work of the Holy Ghost is not measured only by experiences, gifts, impressions, or extraordinary manifestations. The Spirit also develops character. Galatians 5:22–23 describes the fruit of the Spirit as love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control. These qualities reveal the transforming work of God within a believer. As women grow in spiritual discernment, the evidence of that growth should also become visible in daily life. Our responses to difficult people, our patience during waiting seasons, our conduct during conflict, our willingness to forgive, our humility when corrected, and our obedience when challenged all reveal areas where the Holy Ghost is working. A person may speak confidently about spiritual experiences while displaying little patience, kindness, humility, or self-control. Spiritual maturity must involve more than what we claim to hear. Our lives should increasingly reflect the character of Christ. The fruit of the Spirit helps us examine our spiritual growth. Are we becoming more loving? Are we developing patience? Are we learning to exercise self-control? Are we responding with gentleness? Are we growing in faithfulness? Are our relationships reflecting the character of Jesus? These questions help us evaluate the transforming work of the Holy Ghost within us. Biblical Example: The Early Church Acts 2 records the outpouring of the Holy Ghost on the Day of Pentecost. The believers received the promise of the Spirit, and the church began to grow through the preaching of the gospel. Following the outpouring, Acts 2:42–47 describes a community devoted to the apostles’ teaching, fellowship, breaking of bread, prayer, generosity, worship, and shared life. The work of the Holy Ghost was evident not only through the initial manifestation but also through the transformed life of the community. Their spiritual experience produced devotion, unity, generosity, worship, and faithfulness. Key Principle: The Holy Ghost develops both spiritual understanding and Christlike character within us. ⸻ Bringing the Lesson Together The Holy Ghost leads us into truth, brings conviction, provides wisdom, develops discernment, and produces spiritual fruit. His work is not separated from Scripture, prayer, relationship, or obedience. The Spirit of God helps us understand the Word, recognize areas requiring correction, exercise wisdom, and remain faithful to the direction of the Lord. As women seeking to grow in spiritual discernment, we must become attentive to the work of the Holy Ghost while remaining grounded in truth. We do not need to manufacture spiritual experiences. We do not need to interpret every feeling as divine direction. We can seek God with confidence, examine our hearts honestly, remain faithful to Scripture, and allow the Holy Ghost to develop spiritual maturity within us. The Spirit leads us toward truth. The Spirit corrects us toward righteousness. The Spirit strengthens us toward obedience. The Spirit develops within us the character of Christ. Our responsibility is to remain yielded, teachable, prayerful, and willing to follow. ⸻ Personal Reflection and Preparation for Class Prayerfully consider the following questions before our gathering: 1.
