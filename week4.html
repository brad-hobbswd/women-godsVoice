A Seven-Week Bible Study for Women · Week 4

Testing What We Hear

Developing Biblical Discernment, Wisdom, and Spiritual Confidence

✦ ❧ ✦

Hear · Discern · Respond

Key Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 5:19–22

Supporting Scripture: 1 John 4:1

Introduction: Not Everything We Hear Should Be Accepted

As we grow in our relationship with God, we may experience moments when a thought, impression, Scripture, dream, prophetic word, or circumstance seems to carry spiritual significance. We may feel strongly that God is directing us toward a particular decision, revealing something important, or bringing clarity to a situation.

Spiritual maturity requires more than being receptive to what we believe God may be saying. We must also learn to examine, test, and discern what we hear.

1 Thessalonians 5:19–22 instructs believers not to quench the Spirit, not to despise prophetic utterances, but to examine everything carefully and hold firmly to what is good.

These instructions reveal a balance. We should not become dismissive of the work of God, nor should we accept every spiritual claim without examination.

Discernment protects us from deception, confusion, manipulation, and decisions driven by personal desires rather than biblical truth.

As women seeking God’s direction, we may encounter spiritual messages concerning relationships, family, ministry, employment, personal decisions, or our future. Some messages may encourage us. Others may challenge us. Some may require time, prayer, and careful examination before we understand how to respond.

God does not require us to abandon wisdom in order to demonstrate faith.

Throughout this lesson, we will learn how to test what we hear, remain grounded in Scripture, recognize the influence of our emotions, seek wise counsel, and respond with humility.

Scripture Is the Standard for Testing What We Hear

The most important foundation for discernment is the Word of God.

Every impression, message, prophecy, dream, interpretation, or perceived direction must be examined through Scripture. God’s voice will never contradict His written Word.

2 Timothy 3:16–17 teaches that Scripture is inspired by God and equips believers for every good work. The Word provides the foundation upon which spiritual understanding and discernment must be built.

A message may sound convincing, feel powerful, or come from someone respected. Those qualities alone do not establish that the message is from God.

We must examine what is being communicated.

Does the message agree with Scripture?

Does the direction encourage obedience to God?

Does the message contradict biblical principles?

Does the message promote sin, dishonesty, manipulation, pride, fear, or compromise?

God’s Word provides the standard by which we evaluate spiritual claims.

Biblical Example

The Bereans

Acts 17:10–12 describes the Bereans, who received the teaching of Paul and Silas with eagerness while examining the Scriptures daily to determine whether the things being taught were true.

Their response demonstrates an important principle. They were receptive to teaching, yet they remained committed to examining Scripture.

They did not reject the message simply because it was unfamiliar. They did not accept the message merely because a respected teacher presented it.

They searched the Scriptures.

Their example encourages us to remain teachable while exercising biblical discernment.

Key Principle

A spiritual message must be examined through the truth of God’s Word.

Test the Source, Not Just the Message

1 John 4:1 instructs believers to test the spirits because not every spiritual claim originates from God.

Discernment requires us to examine the source of what we hear. A person may sincerely believe they are communicating a message from God while misunderstanding an impression, interpreting an experience incorrectly, or allowing personal opinions to influence what they say.

Sincerity does not automatically establish accuracy.

We must also recognize that spiritual language can be used to influence, pressure, control, or manipulate others.

Statements such as “God told me you must do this” should never remove another person’s responsibility to pray, examine Scripture, exercise wisdom, and seek confirmation.

No individual should use a claimed revelation to control another person’s relationships, finances, decisions, personal boundaries, or spiritual life.

God’s direction does not require manipulation.

Healthy spiritual counsel encourages a person to seek God, examine the Word, exercise wisdom, and respond willingly.

Biblical Example

The False Prophets

Throughout Scripture, God’s people were warned about false prophets who spoke messages that did not originate from Him.

Jeremiah 23:16 warns against listening to messages that arise from the imagination and personal desires of those speaking rather than from the Lord.

The warning demonstrates that spiritual language does not guarantee spiritual truth.

A message may sound encouraging, impressive, or authoritative while lacking biblical foundation.

We must evaluate the message according to truth rather than presentation.

Key Principle

Spiritual authority is never established by confidence, charisma, emotional intensity, or the use of God’s name.

Examine Your Own Heart and Emotions

Discernment requires honesty about the influences within our own hearts.

Our desires, fears, disappointments, expectations, and emotions can affect how we interpret what we hear.

Sometimes we may desire a particular outcome so strongly that we begin searching for confirmation everywhere.

We may interpret a coincidence as a sign, assume an opportunity represents God’s approval, or believe a particular person is part of God’s plan simply because the situation feels meaningful.

Emotions are part of our human experience. God created us with the ability to feel joy, sorrow, concern, love, compassion, and excitement. However, emotions are not the final authority for determining God’s direction.

We can acknowledge our feelings without allowing our feelings to replace Scripture and wisdom.

Before accepting an impression, we should prayerfully examine our motives.

Am I seeking God’s will?

Am I hoping God will approve something I have already decided?

Am I responding to fear?

Am I feeling pressured to make a decision quickly?

Am I interpreting this situation through disappointment, loneliness, excitement, or anxiety?

Am I willing to accept an answer different from the outcome I desire?

Honest examination helps us approach God with humility.

Biblical Example

Jonah

Jonah 1:1–3 records the Lord directing Jonah to go to Nineveh. Jonah chose to travel in the opposite direction.

Jonah had received clear direction, yet his personal attitude and unwillingness to obey influenced his response.

Later chapters reveal Jonah’s struggle with God’s mercy toward Nineveh.

Jonah’s story reminds us that hearing direction and willingly responding to direction are not the same.

Our personal desires can influence our willingness to accept what God is asking of us.

Key Principle

Discernment requires a surrendered heart willing to accept God’s direction even when personal desires differ.

Seek Confirmation, Wisdom, and Godly Counsel

Some decisions require time, prayer, wisdom, and confirmation.

Not every impression requires an immediate response. Not every opportunity requires a quick decision. Spiritual maturity includes recognizing when we need to slow down and seek greater understanding.

Proverbs 11:14 teaches that safety is found through an abundance of counselors. Godly counsel can provide perspective, help us recognize blind spots, and encourage us to examine decisions carefully.

However, counsel must be evaluated through Scripture and spiritual wisdom.

A counselor should not manipulate us, pressure us, or claim authority over our personal decisions through supposed revelation.

Wise counsel encourages prayer, biblical understanding, personal responsibility, and spiritual maturity.

Confirmation may come through a clearer understanding of Scripture, wise counsel, circumstances, prayer, or the development of peace and clarity over time. Confirmation should never be forced by searching for repeated coincidences or demanding that God provide signs according to our preferences.

We must be careful not to create our own confirmation.

A decision that requires dishonesty, compromise, or disobedience does not become God’s direction simply because several circumstances appear favorable.

Biblical Example

The Jerusalem Council

Acts 15 records a significant decision within the early church concerning Gentile believers.

The leaders gathered, discussed the matter, examined what God had done, considered Scripture, listened to testimony, and reached a conclusion.

The decision was not made through individual preference alone. It involved spiritual leadership, biblical understanding, testimony, discussion, and discernment.

The example demonstrates the importance of examining significant matters carefully within a community of faith.

Key Principle

Godly confirmation strengthens discernment, but confirmation must remain grounded in Scripture and wisdom.

Respond With Humility, Patience, and Obedience

Testing what we hear should not make us fearful of seeking God. Discernment is intended to strengthen our confidence in truth and help us respond faithfully.

We will not always understand everything immediately.

There may be seasons when we receive direction but do not yet understand how circumstances will unfold. There may be moments when an impression requires further examination. There may be situations when we need to acknowledge that we misunderstood something.

Humility allows us to remain teachable.

We must be willing to say:

“I need more time to pray.”

“I want to examine this through Scripture.”

“I may have misunderstood.”

“I need wise counsel.”

“I do not yet have clarity.”

These responses demonstrate maturity rather than weakness.

God is not threatened by sincere questions. He invites us to seek wisdom.

James 1:5 teaches that those who lack wisdom may ask God, who gives generously.

Our responsibility is to seek wisdom, remain faithful, and obey what God has clearly established through His Word.

Biblical Example

Paul and His Journey

Acts 16:6–10 describes Paul and his companions encountering circumstances that redirected their missionary journey. The Holy Ghost prevented them from entering certain regions, and Paul later received a vision concerning Macedonia.

The group considered the vision and understood that God was calling them to preach the gospel there.

Their response involved discernment, consideration, and action.

The passage demonstrates that God’s direction may unfold through a process. His leading may redirect our plans, open opportunities, or require us to move forward in faith.

We must remain willing to follow while continuing to exercise spiritual wisdom.

Key Principle

Mature discernment leads to humility, faithful obedience, and confidence in God’s wisdom rather than fear or presumption.

Bringing the Lesson Together

Testing what we hear is an essential part of spiritual maturity.

We should remain open to the work of God while examining spiritual messages carefully. Scripture must remain our foundation. The source of a message must be considered. Our own emotions and desires must be examined. Wise counsel and confirmation may provide additional perspective. Our response must remain marked by humility and obedience.

We do not need to accept every impression as divine direction.

We do not need to reject every spiritual experience out of fear.

We can remain prayerful, receptive, discerning, and grounded in truth.

The purpose of discernment is not to create suspicion. The purpose is to protect our spiritual lives, strengthen our understanding, and help us follow the Lord faithfully.

As women of God, we can confidently seek His direction while remaining committed to examining everything through His Word.

Hear His voice. Discern His leading. Respond with obedience.

Personal Reflection and Preparation for Class

Prayerfully consider the following questions before our gathering:

  • Why does Scripture need to remain the standard for testing what we hear?
  • What is the difference between sincerely believing something is from God and having biblical confirmation that the message is true?
  • How can personal desires, fear, loneliness, excitement, or anxiety influence our discernment?
  • Why is godly counsel important when making significant decisions?
  • How can spiritual language be misused to manipulate or control others?
  • What does humility look like when we realize we may have misunderstood God’s direction?
  • Are there areas in your life where you need to slow down, pray, examine Scripture, and seek wisdom before making a decision?

Closing Prayer

Lord, thank You for Your truth and for giving us Your Word as the foundation of our faith.

Teach us to exercise spiritual discernment with wisdom, humility, and confidence. Help us remain receptive to Your leading while examining everything through Scripture.

Protect our hearts from deception, confusion, manipulation, fear, and personal desires that may influence our understanding.

Give us wisdom when decisions are difficult. Help us recognize when we need patience, prayer, confirmation, or godly counsel.

Teach us to remain humble when we do not understand and willing to receive correction when we have misunderstood.

May we become women who are grounded in Your Word, sensitive to the Holy Ghost, wise in our decisions, and faithful in our obedience.

Help us trust You completely and follow Your direction with confidence.

In Jesus’ name, Amen.
